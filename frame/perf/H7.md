## 1. Does the zigzag follow the end time? No.

**Alignment, to the refresh.** A refresh with no frame (XRF wake-up step > 1) shows black on both panels.
Panel refresh n (round(pts x 120)) shows the frame of wake-up refresh r = n + K, counted from the first XRF
line by summed wake-up steps (no drift). Launch 1: K = 458; black-vs-missing correlation by K: 456 0.33,
457 0.62, **458 1.00**, 459 0.62, 460 0.31 (the gaps are 3 refreshes wide), two anchors 44 s apart (r
932-934 -> panels 474-476, r 6160-6162 -> 5702-5704), and the retained repeat r 6163 (f=0) lands on panel
5705, pixel-identical to 5701 (r 6159), as a repeat must. Launch 2: K = 455 +-1 (fewer, messier gaps).
In time: video t + 3.8 s = XRF w; video t + about 4.5 s = `vr pacing` t (not the 5.9 s of the sixth
session: the recorder starts at a different point after the game each time; take K from black refreshes).
Generic signals (e, f=0, late) correlate with no panel measure at any offset (|r| < 0.2, flat).

**The zigzag is real.** Launch 1, right eye, refreshes 3839-3847: three textured 48x48 patches tracked at
full resolution: +3.9, -11.3, +4.0, -11.0 px (480-px units) per refresh against motion.txt's +3.3, -11.0,
+3.6, -10.9. Every second refresh sits about 7.5 px off a smooth -3.5 px/refresh track: an image placed
two refreshes of motion away, then back. Not pixel-identical to an earlier one (stale: 0.2 %).

- **e: no.** In launch 1's race 78 % of frames ended within 1 ms of the wake-up (e p50 0.11 ms) and the
  right eye zigzagged on 84 % of those refreshes. Neither eye's rate rises with e in launch 2 (e p50
  2.2 ms). e of the frame shown, of the frame before or after, the change of e, and e straddling 1, 2 or 4
  ms each explain under 4 % of the variance.
- **f: no.** Launch 2 right eye: 68 % on fresh frames, 68 % on repeats, 71 % right after a repeat.
- **p: no refresh-level effect.** Both pairs alike in launch 1; where a repeat flips pair against refresh
  parity the zigzag phase followed the pair 4 times of 7. The pair count changes the shape: with 2 pairs
  clean +-2-refresh jumps with period 2, with 6 pairs irregular jumps of 1 to 8 refreshes (only 8 to 11 % at
  a pair's own reuse distance).
- **What does predict it: the eye and race against menus.** Same frames, same e, f, p: right 83 %, left

So the premise of this lane (frames ended late after the wake-up are shown inconsistently and that is the
doubling) does not hold for this session: launch 1 was already ended at the wake-up for most of its race.
The sawtooth is real (launch 2: e p50 2.2 ms, p90 7.5 ms; launch 1 in its worse seconds), and sections 2-4
remove it, but on this evidence the doubling has another cause, specific to the eye, present with any end
time and any pair count. Candidates not tested here: what SteamVR does with the right eye's layer (its pose
or image) when it composes; a per-eye difference in our own submission; the race's scene motion (a game
frame is two refreshes, the size of the jump) against the head's.

## 2. The fix for the sawtooth: sets rendered ahead (the new default of `pipelined`)

**Why the old loop drifted.** It had one set in flight: the set for refresh k+1 was located and published
only after frame k was ended, and frame k was ended when its own set finished on the GPU (waiting up to
the deadline, 1 ms before the next wake-up). A set costs encode (about 2.5 ms; behind a game frame's
encode on the frame worker when one is running) plus GPU (about 5 ms p50 in the worse race seconds,
queued behind the game frame's own GPU work): more than a refresh. So each frame ended later after its
wake-up by the excess (e(k+1) = e(k) + cost - 8.33 ms; the race's +1.7 ms a refresh), until a set missed
the deadline, the cycle repeated the retained layer, and the next set, started at once, ended near the
wake-up again: the 0 to 10 ms sawtooth with a repeat every 4 to 5 refreshes. Not the frame worker
interleaving as such, but the serial chain: the start of a set waited for the end of the last.

**The new cycle** (`openxr_integration.cpp` AheadCycle, backend `*AheadSet*` in `openxr_vulkan_win32.cpp`):

1. xrWaitFrame, xrBeginFrame.
2. Among the sets due by this refresh, take the newest whose GPU work is finished (a non-blocking poll of
   the timeline semaphore); end the frame at once with it (its own poses, this cycle's display time),
   else with the retained layer. Never wait inside the cycle. A set due now and not finished counts late;
   if it finishes later it is shown on a later refresh unless a newer set is ready first, then it is
   released unshown (`dropped=`).
3. Start the set for the display time two refreshes ahead (this cycle's predicted time + 2 periods),
   located now for that time. Aurora takes one set at a time, so the previous set must have reached
   Aurora's queue.Submit first (it has, normally: it was published a refresh ago; the wait is bounded by
   this cycle's deadline, else no set starts this refresh).

So each set has the rest of its starting refresh and all of the next (about 16 ms) for encode plus GPU,
one set is on the GPU while the next is encoded, and the end of a frame no longer depends on any set's
timing: it is the wake-up plus xrBeginFrame, the poll, two releases and xrEndFrame. With the race's
measured costs (encode 2.5, GPU 5 p50, 11 p99 including queueing) a set finishes 3 to 9 ms before its
wake-up; only a set over about 15 ms total misses its refresh. Pose prediction is one refresh longer
than before (located about 42 ms before display instead of 33; SteamVR's own lead is 25 of those). The
scene clock (interpolation) learns the new lead by itself. `KARTFRAME_AHEAD_START_US` (default 0)
delays each start after the wake-up for less prediction at the cost of margin.

**Three eye pairs.** Two sets in flight plus the pair on display. A set never writes the displayed pair,
so a set that is overtaken can be released unshown: a release makes that image its swapchain's newest,
which for the displayed pair would change what the retained layer shows. With two pairs the second set
would have to use the displayed pair. Pairs are reused first in, first out: with three, a pair is written
again right after it left the display (as the old two-pair alternation did); `KARTFRAME_EYE_PAIRS=4..8`
lets each rest longer. The old loop's default goes back to 2 (six were worse on the headset). The menu
environment image travels with its set the same way (three of those too, made as needed).

**Switches.** `KARTFRAME_PIPELINED=wait` restores the old loop (with its own pair default, 2).
`KARTFRAME_PACING=inline` is unchanged. Sets ahead need the timeline semaphore (polled); without it the
backend keeps the old loop and the log says so. Log: "OpenXR pipelined pacing: sets rendered two
refreshes ahead ..." and "OpenXR eye swapchain pairs: 3 (sets rendered ahead ...)"; the pacing line's mode
reads `pipelined` (new) or `pipelined-wait` (old) and ends in `dropped=<n> end_late=<n>` (finished sets
released unshown; xrEndFrame calls more than 1 ms after the wake-up). session-report.py shows both.

## 3. The mock scores it

`xrmock` now logs every xrEndFrame against the runtime's own wake-up (the refresh grid, so a late
xrWaitFrame call cannot hide lateness) and prints at the end of the session:
"xrmock: end after wake-up (ms, from the runtime's wake-up): N frames, p50, p99, max; histogram <0.5,
0.5-1, 1-1.5, 1.5-2, 2-4, 4-6, 6-8.3, more; later than 1 ms K (P %)". `KF_MOCKRUN_END_GATE=<percent>` makes
mockrun FAIL above that share (2 separates the two loops below: old 3.0 to 55 %, new 0.4 to 1.0 %).
It also prints a late-end model ("xrmock: late-end model ..."): a frame ended more than
`MOCK_XR_LATE_END_MS` (default 1.5) after its wake-up is taken for the refresh after its display time
(PrimeFrame's H2 reading of SteamVR), each vsync shows the newest frame taken for it, and "zigzag" counts
vsyncs whose pose step differs from the last by 2 or more (a 0 next to a 2). It is a model, not a measure:
section 1 shows the headset's zigzag does not follow the end time, and the model makes no claim about it.
lane J's compositor models (spec, lazyrel, lazyacq) are unchanged.
