# H9 (lane O): what KartFrame hands SteamVR, against GalaxyFrame

GalaxyFrame (native Vulkan, 120 Hz, both eyes from one locate every refresh, two eye swapchain sets,
images released after a fence) was clean on the same headset on 2026-10-14 (30 s of head turns), while
KartFrame doubles on head turns in every one of nine sessions. So SteamVR and the Frame are fine; this page
lists every difference in what the two apps hand SteamVR, as SteamVR logged it (section 1), as the mock
runtime sees the calls (section 2) and in the Vulkan hand-over (section 3), what was switched to
GalaxyFrame's way (section 4) and the next session (section 5). Scratch: `$HOME/.cache/kf-laneO/h9/`.

Reading it:
- The two real hand-over differences SteamVR shows are the **pixel format** (BGRA against RGBA: the
  client imports both as dmabufs with modifiers, both compressed, judging by the metadata after each image,
  but the compositor samples a B8G8R8A8 image on another path than R8G8B8A8 for all we know) and the
  **usage** (TRANSFER_DST against SAMPLED; it can change the modifier the client picks). The size
  (1728 against 1555) and the swapchain count are differences too.
- "ApplicationTime GPU" is SteamVR's time between its own GPU marks in xrBeginFrame and xrEndFrame:
  GalaxyFrame submits its eye work between them, KartFrame's frame worker submits it at other times. Not a
  fault, but SteamVR sees a 1 ms app.
- Nothing in either log names a per-eye problem; SteamVR's counters are as clean for KartFrame as for
  GalaxyFrame (0.4 to 0.6 % reprojected).

## 2. The OpenXR calls per frame (both apps against the same mock)

`xrmock` has a detail trace now (`MOCK_XR_TRACE_DETAIL=1`): every line ends in "@ms after the frame's
scheduled wake-up", plus the reference spaces with their poses, every xrLocateViews (space, display time
against the frame's predicted one), xrWaitSwapchainImage's timeout, and per xrEndFrame the blend mode,
display time, next chains, and per layer its space, flags, rects, array indices, fields of view and the
locate its poses came from. GalaxyFrame's installed binary ran against KartFrame's mock in a scratch home
(`gfmock.sh`: `GALAXYFRAME_HOME` scratch, the game data linked read-only, `PETARI_GFX=vk`, 30 s,
`TU_DEBUG=sysmem`); KartFrame the scripted Grand Prix (`frame/perf/hudrace.sh`, 90 s). Steady frames
(GalaxyFrame f2500, KartFrame race f9000, baseline build 3337d22):

| | KartFrame (baseline) | GalaxyFrame |
| --- | --- | --- |
| order in a refresh | wait @0.07, begin @0.18-0.33, release L, R, **xrEndFrame @0.29-0.53**, then locate for **+2 refreshes**, acquire + wait L, R (Aurora renders the set on its frame worker) | wait @0.08, begin @0.21, **locate for +1 refresh**, acquire + wait L, R, record and submit both eyes, release the previous set L, R @0.48-0.76, release 3 UI quads, **locate for this frame's own display time** @0.82-0.86, acquire UI quads, **xrEndFrame @0.91-1.01** |
| last xrLocateViews before xrEndFrame | the one for the set two refreshes ahead (made the refresh before) | one for the frame's own display time, right before the end |
| reference spaces | VIEW, LOCAL, and a recentred LOCAL (pose 0, 1.6, 0 and a yaw) for locates and layers | the same three (LOCAL twice, VIEW, then the recentred LOCAL) |
| poses submitted | the set's own locate, for the end's display time (+-0.003 refreshes of SteamVR-like jitter) | the same |
| xrWaitSwapchainImage | infinite timeout | infinite timeout |
| xrEndFrame | blend OPAQUE, display time = the frame's predicted time, no next chain; one projection layer, flags 0, recentred LOCAL, each view its own swapchain, rect 0,0 1728x1728, array 0, no next chain (no depth) | the same, 1555x1555; plus three quad layers (flags BLEND_TEXTURE_SOURCE_ALPHA) |
| ends after the wake-up (mock) | p50 0.47 ms, 1.6 % over 1 ms | p50 0.96 ms, 42.6 % over 1 ms |
| swapchains | 3 eye pairs (6), 2 environment (3456x1728), usage 0x11; per pair left and right image indices out of step (the menus' screen uses only the left image) | 4 eye swapchains, 8 UI, usage 0x21; indices in step |
| extensions (mock) | vulkan_enable2, convert_timespec_time, frame_controller, display_refresh_rate, performance_settings | opengl_es_enable, MNDX_egl_enable, vulkan_enable2, frame_controller, display_refresh_rate, performance_settings, eye_gaze_interaction (the mock offers no META/space_warp) |

Nothing GalaxyFrame submits is missing from KartFrame's projection layer (no depth info, no flags, no
composition settings: GalaxyFrame's layer settings and SpaceWarp are off on Vulkan). The differences are
the order (GalaxyFrame acquires and records the next set before it releases and ends; KartFrame releases
and ends first), the extra locate for the frame's own time before the end, the lead of the set's locate
(+1 against +2 refreshes; KartFrame's old one-set loop, `KARTFRAME_PIPELINED=wait`, located for +1 like
GalaxyFrame and doubled the same, H7 launch 1), when in the refresh the end comes (KartFrame earlier) and
the quad layers beside the eyes.

## 3. The Vulkan hand-over

GalaxyFrame: `platform/src/xr/xr_gfx_vk.cpp`, `gx/vk_renderer.cpp` (transitionImage, Barriers). KartFrame:
`openxr_vulkan_win32.cpp`, `aurora-main/lib/webgpu/vulkan_win32_interop.cpp` (Encode, Submitted), the Dawn
patch (`AuroraDawnVulkanWrap`, `AuroraDawnVulkanRelease`, Dawn's `TextureVk.cpp` barrier rules).

| | KartFrame | GalaxyFrame |
| --- | --- | --- |
| format, views | **B8G8R8A8_SRGB**, one sRGB view, no MUTABLE_FORMAT (sRGB sink) | **R8G8B8A8_SRGB**, one sRGB view, no MUTABLE_FORMAT |
| usage | **COLOR_ATTACHMENT, TRANSFER_DST** | **COLOR_ATTACHMENT, SAMPLED** |
| how the eye gets in | a Dawn render pass (LOAD_OP_CLEAR, STORE) with a full-screen triangle that loads Aurora's eye texture and writes it through the sRGB view | its own dynamic-rendering passes composite the eye straight into the image |
| barrier at the first write after xrWaitSwapchainImage | Dawn's tracked state: COLOR_ATTACHMENT_OPTIMAL to COLOR_ATTACHMENT_OPTIMAL, src COLOR_ATTACHMENT_OUTPUT / COLOR_ATTACHMENT_READ+WRITE, dst the same, queue families IGNORED | `assumeImageLayout`: src **ALL_COMMANDS / MEMORY_WRITE**, dst COLOR_ATTACHMENT_OUTPUT / COLOR_ATTACHMENT_READ+WRITE, old layout COLOR_ATTACHMENT_OPTIMAL (UNDEFINED where the pass discards), queue families IGNORED |
| barrier before the release | COLOR_ATTACHMENT_OPTIMAL to COLOR_ATTACHMENT_OPTIMAL, COLOR_ATTACHMENT_OUTPUT, COLOR_ATTACHMENT_READ+WRITE to the same, IGNORED (appended by `AuroraDawnVulkanRelease`, flushed in its own submit) | none when the image is already COLOR_ATTACHMENT_OPTIMAL after the pass (else a transition to it), IGNORED |
| ownership transfer to EXTERNAL / FOREIGN | none | none |
| GPU finished before the release | timeline semaphore signalled after the set, waited before every release (mock: 0 of 21182 released images changed afterwards) | timeline value per submit, waited before the release (and re-checked in it) |
| command buffers | one Dawn command buffer for both eyes (and the test stamp), one more submit for the release barriers | one command buffer for both eyes |
| GPU writes after the release barrier | none: the stamp (tests only) and the sink draw are in the set's encoder before it; the release submit holds only barriers | none |
| reads of swapchain images | never | none for the eyes in play (SAMPLED is there for UI mips and debug shots) |
| an image on display rewritten | no: a set never writes the pair on display (lane K); mock spec and lazyrel models 100 % own poses | no: two sets, the one on display is not acquired again until the next is released |
| queue | one queue; SteamVR's client submits on it inside our begin, end, acquire and release calls, under Dawn's device guard | one queue, one thread |
| memory (SteamVR) | 1728x1728x4 + 56 KiB (compressed: UBWC metadata) | 1600x1568x4 + 52 KiB (compressed) |

Memory visibility for the compositor (a compressed image read stale by a foreign reader): both apps give
SteamVR the same guarantee, the image finished on the GPU and in COLOR_ATTACHMENT_OPTIMAL, without a queue
family release to EXTERNAL and without a flush of their own; SteamVR's client does the hand-off on our
queue after our release. If that left a compressed image partly stale, GalaxyFrame would show it too, and
a stale image would show old pixels: lane N's search (below) finds the doubled refreshes are not pixel
matches of older (or newer) images. KartFrame's release barrier is the stronger of the two; GalaxyFrame's
first-write barrier is (it waits for ALL_COMMANDS and MEMORY_WRITE, so it orders after whatever SteamVR's
client recorded at the acquire). Making Dawn do that needs a new Dawn entry point (a rebuild of Dawn);
not done here, it is the next candidate if the format does not decide it (section 5).

## 4. What changed (switches, default GalaxyFrame's way)

- `KARTFRAME_XR_FORMAT=rgba|bgra` (default rgba): the eye pairs are R8G8B8A8_SRGB (VkFormat 43) whenever
  the sRGB sink is on and SteamVR offers 43 (it does). Aurora still renders BGRA; the sink's draw writes
  any channel order, so it costs nothing. The menus' screen image (the left image of a pair) went in by a
  copy before; with RGBA it takes the same draw. The menu environment and settings panel swapchains keep
  Aurora's BGRA (they take copies). `bgra` is the old format; the copy and blit sinks keep BGRA.
- `KARTFRAME_XR_USAGE=galaxy|kart` (default galaxy): eye swapchains COLOR_ATTACHMENT | SAMPLED (0x21), with
  TRANSFER_DST only where a copy can still reach an eye image (BGRA eyes, the test stamp, the copy sinks);
  `kart` is the old COLOR_ATTACHMENT | TRANSFER_DST (0x11).
- `KARTFRAME_END_LOCATE=1|0` (default 1): one xrLocateViews for the frame's own display time between the
  set's release and xrEndFrame, as GalaxyFrame's last call before its end (result unused; eye-set frames
  only, not the menus; through the runtime's dispatch). About 0.01 ms.
- `KARTFRAME_XR_EXTENSIONS=galaxy` (default KartFrame's list): exactly GalaxyFrame's extension request
  (its GLES binding extensions beside Vulkan's, XR_FB_space_warp, XR_META_recommended_layer_resolution,
  XR_META_performance_metrics, XR_EXT_eye_gaze_interaction; no XR_KHR_convert_timespec_time, no other
  controllers' profiles, no hand tracking). Not the default: it drops hand steering and the other
  headsets' controller profiles. Without the clock extension, display times read XrTime as
  CLOCK_MONOTONIC (SteamVR's clock); that choice is now made once at the first frame (a stalled frame
  switched it to the anchored fallback before, which the mock's stamp check caught in a dump stall).
- Log line: "OpenXR Vulkan swapchains ready: VkFormat 50 (Aurora 44), eye swapchains VkFormat 43 usage
  0x21 (KARTFRAME_XR_FORMAT=rgba, KARTFRAME_XR_USAGE=galaxy), end locate on (KARTFRAME_END_LOCATE), ...";
  "OpenXR extensions asked for: ...".
- Not switched: the order (acquire and record the next set before the release and end: sets ahead keep
  three pairs in rotation and would need a fourth), the +1 lead (tested: the old loop), the eye size (1555
  is GalaxyFrame's resolution 0.9; 1728 is the floor here), the quad layers (none in KartFrame's race),
  the first-write barrier (Dawn).

## Lane N's future-lag analysis (uncommitted, `$HOME/.cache/kf-laneN/`): verdict

Lane N asked whether the doubled refreshes are images newer than the frame's poses (SteamVR reading an eye
after a newer release) as well as older ones. On the headset recordings (`cross-*.txt`, `cadence-*.txt`:
best match of each refresh among the 8 before and the 8 after, per eye, at rest, slow and turning) the
matches are symmetric in past and future (for example 20261014-111622 right eye turning: -7 17.4 %, -5
15.0 %, +5 10.8 %, +7 12.8 %), and pixel-exact matches with another refresh are rare and as often future
as past (0 to 4.7 %, right eye at rest 9 past / 3 future). In the mock, a lazy compositor shows newer
images only if it reads the eyes after the vsync (3 pairs: 96 to 97 % newer when read 0 to 5 ms after;
none when read before), and with 6 pairs almost never; yet 6 and 8 pairs were worse on the headset. So the
doubled image is neither an older nor a newer swapchain image: the content is right for its set and placed
off, which is what H7 measured (not pixel-identical, two refreshes of head motion). That points at how
SteamVR takes our layer, not at which image it reads; the hand-over differences above are what is left.

One older data point: the third session (H3, 20261013-132235, lane G's build) is the only KartFrame
session the owner called free of doubling. It ended every layer with its poses' locate time (lane G's
`KARTFRAME_END_TIME=locate`) and had 24 % late sets; its frame rate "felt really bad", which may have
hidden the doubling. The format, usage, order and extensions were as in every other session.
