## 1. Doubling on head turns: lane H's full-view fades took textured composites

Lane H's R1 (f127ea4) drew every blended full-frame 2D rectangle after the scene across the whole eye,
mapped from frame space, i.e. pinned to the head. Untextured ones are fades (black, white flashes,
wipes); textured ones composite a picture of the scene (the ghost from its offscreen copy, the player's
character). Pinned to the head, such a picture doubles against the world on every head turn. The
session's log has the textured, depth-tested full-frame rectangle (blend 1, depth func LESS, 2 TEV
stages, 1 texgen) that R1 made full-view, one a frame in the Time Trial.

Fix (lane/I-fixes 7046395, lane/I 65e6fce): a full-view fade needs no texgens. Textured full-frame quads
take their pre-lane-H path (the virtual screen, or the framebuffer-effect path when they sample a fresh
EFB copy). `Full-frame 2D draw class: blend B logic L texgens N -> <path>` names each class once. Mock
(demo race and a scripted Grand Prix, head sway +-0.3 rad): only "blend 1 logic 0/15 texgens 0" go
across the eye (one a frame: a black quad blended by destination alpha, per eye pixel, so it cannot
double); "blend 1 logic 15/3 texgens 1", "texgens 2" are textured and now stay on the screen. Not
reproduced: a real ghost (no scripted route to a Time Trial). A textured composite of an offscreen
render was never stereo-correct (before lane H either): it is flat on the screen. Making the ghost
3D needs its own draws replayed per eye (follow-up).

## 2. Laser on the menu screen: SteamVR ignores our layer order

With "OpenXR menu environment layer ready (3456x1728, over the screen)" the ray and its mark, drawn in
the environment projection layer over the screen's quad, still did not show on the screen in the
headset, while the mock (which draws layers in submission order) showed them. So SteamVR on the Frame
evidently composites the projection layer before (under) the quad layer whatever order xrEndFrame lists
them in. Nothing may depend on the order of a projection layer against a quad layer there.

Fix (lane/I): the pointer's target marks (a dark-rimmed ring in the hand's colour with a white centre,
both hands when both aim at the screen) are drawn into the screen quad's own image, on its surface:
visible and stereo-correct under any layer order. The environment layer goes under the screen quad by
default again (KARTFRAME_MENU_ENV=over: lane H's order). The ray now stops at the screen's edge where
the quad covers it. Mock dump (menus, MOCK_XR_HANDS=1, t=35 s): both marks on the screen image.
